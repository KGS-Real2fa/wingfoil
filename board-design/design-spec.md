# 2.0 m / 120 L downwind pump board: design spec (step 1)

Rider: 95 kg plus wetsuit and wing (about 100 kg on the board). Foils: Armstrong HA 2450 or F-One 1950.
Build: hot-wire cut EPS core, vacuum-bagged carbon/epoxy skins, basalt on the rails only.

All numbers are generated by `board_model.py`; change a parameter there and rerun `templates.py` to regenerate everything.

## Main dimensions

| | |
|---|---|

| Max thickness | 154 mm at the stringer, from 650 to 1050 mm from tail |
| Volume | 120.0 L (integrated from the sections) |
| Tail width | 342 mm, squared tail with a hard release edge |
| Nose rocker | 112 mm at the tip; starts lifting at 1000 mm from tail |
| Tail kick | none: dead straight run from the transom to 1000 mm |
| Core blank needed | 2050 × 580 × 170 mm EPS |

## Shape choices for pumping

- **Dead straight run from the transom to 1000 mm from tail.** That is the whole foil and standing zone, so the board breaks free with a straight run when you pump up and has nothing to plough on touchdowns.
- **Long, low nose rocker.** Gives 112 mm of lift at the tip so the nose rides over the back of bumps instead of sticking.
- **Chined (bevelled) bottom.** The flat bottom runs 82% of the half-width at the tail and narrows to 40% at the nose, with a chine rising to 10% to 34% of thickness. The wetted surface at take-off stays small, and touchdowns are softened.
- **Boxy rails with a vertical rail band** up to 40% to 50% of thickness, then a flat deck. This keeps the volume low and wide, close to the foil, rather than piling it up into a thick dome.
- **Skiff stern (added 2026-10-15).** Square transom, zero tail kick, and hard chine edges from the transom to 500 mm (fillet radius 0), softening to 4 mm at 900 mm and 14 mm at the nose. Flow leaves the transom and the aft chines tangent, as on a 49er or I14, instead of wrapping round a radius and pulling the tail down.
- **Soft-V nose forward of 1300 mm**, rising to 9 degrees of deadrise at the tip, with a parabolic (not sharp) keel line for quiet touchdowns.

At 120 L on 2.0 m the board needs some thickness. I chose 560 mm wide over 540 mm so it can be 154 mm thick instead of 159 mm, which keeps the deck lower and closer to the foil.

## Foil tracks

- Dual tracks at 90 mm centres (45 mm each side of the stringer), 14 inch (356 mm) long (changed from 10 inch on 2026-10-16 for more trim range across the two foils).
- They run from 582 to 938 mm from the tail, centred at 760 mm (38% of length), in a 150 × 420 mm high-density block from 550 to 970 mm.
- Start with the mast plate in the middle of the track for both foils and adjust by feel. The HA 2450 usually needs the mast a little further back than the 1950, but that is a rule of thumb I inferred, not a measured figure.
- Check that your Armstrong plate (A+ track plate) and F-One plate fit a 90 mm M8 track before you buy the boxes.

## Hot-wire sequence

1. Glue the rocker templates to 3 mm MDF and fix one to each side of the blank. Cut the deck line, then the bottom line.
2. Fix the outline template (flip it for the other half) on the deck and the bottom. Cut the outline.
3. Hand-shape the chine, rails and deck crown with a sanding block. Check against the station templates as you go.
4. Rout the track box pockets after shaping, before laminating.

## Bottom shape: double tunnels considered (2026-10-15)

Karl asked about an AC75-style double-tunnel bottom. Verdict: not for this board. Reasoning:

- **Speed regime.** With 100 kg on the foil and CL of 0.6 to 0.8, take-off is about 6 to 7 kt on the HA 2450 and 7 to 8 kt on the 1950 (Froude number 0.7 to 0.9 on a 2.0 m hull, hull speed is 3.4 kt). Pump-up happens entirely in the semi-displacement hump, where wave drag from the volume distribution along the length dominates. Nothing in that regime rewards a tunnel: tunnels only pay off when air can get into them, at planing speeds (15 kt plus) that this board never sees with its bottom in the water.
- **What the AC75 shape is for.** The bustle (the narrow central keel) seals the gap under the mainsail for aerodynamic end-plating, and it gives a narrow, low-drag wetted strip on touchdowns. The hollows either side are in the air when foiling. A wing board gets no end-plate benefit, and the touchdown benefit comes from the narrow spine, not from the tunnels.
- **Friction is not the problem.** Shallow tunnels (6 mm deep, 150 mm wide) add only about 1 mm to a 560 mm wetted perimeter; even 25 mm deep adds 17 mm (3%). Nobody loses the board over that.
- **What tunnels would cost.** (1) Tail release: tunnels running out through the tail hold water on the bottom as the board breaks free, which is the moment that matters most for pumping. (2) Lift at the hump: a flat plate at Froude 0.7 gives the most dynamic lift per wetted area; concaves move lift aft and reduce it. (3) Roll: two longitudinal ridges resist banking the board into a turn. (4) The track boxes need a flat 120 mm plate area at the stringer, so the tunnels would have to start outboard of 70 mm either side. (5) Build: a hot wire cannot cut longitudinal concaves, so they are hand-shaped, and vacuum-bagged carbon tends to bridge the ridges.
- **What does transfer.** A slightly rounded, soft-V nose section forward of 1400 mm gives AC75-style soft touchdowns without hurting the flat pump zone. The chined flat bottom from 300 to 1000 mm stays.

Option if wanted: a shallow double concave, 4 to 5 mm deep, from 400 to 1200 mm, fading to flat 200 mm before the tail, with a 140 mm flat spine under the tracks. Expect a small gain in tracking while paddling, a small loss in release, and about a day of extra shaping.

## Stern: skiff-style separation (2026-10-15)

Karl asked for a sharper stern like a 49er or I14. Done in the model:

- Zero tail kick: the bottom run is straight from x = 0 to 1000 mm, so the flow leaves the transom tangent to the run. The 5 mm kick in the first version is gone (a kick is a surfboard turning feature and only costs drag here).
- Hard chines aft: the two bottom-edge corners have zero fillet radius from the transom to 500 mm, then soften gradually (4 mm at 900, 10 mm at 1400, 14 mm at the nose). Laminate the aft chines with the basalt rail band wrapped over a sharp core edge and keep the sanding block off it.
- Square transom with a sharp perimeter edge all the way round.
- Aft bottom stays flat (49er, not I14): maximum dynamic lift per wetted area in the hump, cleanest release. V aft would cost lift and release for no gain.

Static check (level trim, from `static_waterline()` in `board_model.py`):

| Load | Waterline above keel | Transom immersion | Freeboard at max thickness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board alone, 7 kg | 13 mm | 13 mm | 141 mm |
| Board + rider, 107 kg | 127 mm | 127 mm | 27 mm |

So at rest with the rider standing the transom is fully immersed (the tail deck is 89 mm high; the tail is awash). That is inherent to 120 L under 107 kg and matters only below about 4 kt (Froude 0.45). From there up, the transom runs dry and the sharp edges do their work, which covers the whole 6 to 8 kt take-off band. The wing pulls the board through the slow band in a few seconds.

## Wing size for 14 kt at 100 kg (2026-10-15)

Estimate, not a measurement. Two methods bracket the answer:

- **Quasi-static drag balance** (no pumping, beam reach, no gusts): take-off at 6 to 7 kt needs about 190 to 200 N of drive (half the weight on the hull at R/Δ 0.16 in the hump, half on the foil at L/D 10, plus mast and fuselage parasitic drag). At 15 kt apparent, with CL 0.9 and 55% of the aero force usable as drive, that asks for about 10 m². Nobody rides that, because nobody takes off quasi-statically.
- **Real take-off** (bear off in a gust at 16 kt, sheet hard with CL 1.1 and 70% drive, rider pumping the foil adds about 50 N): the same balance gives about 4 m². That is the floor, reachable only with good pumping and a gust.

Field practice for 95 to 100 kg riders on a 2000 to 2500 cm² high-aspect foil sits between the two: 6 m² is the normal 14 kt wing, 7 m² if the water is flat-ish and the rider wants it easy, 5 m² for a strong pumper in gusty 14 plus.

Recommendation: **6 m²** with the HA 2450; with the 1950 the take-off speed rises from 6.1 to 6.8 kt and the drag by about 8%, so 6 m² still works but is marginal in the lulls, and 7 m² is the comfortable pairing. For downwinding you want the smallest wing that gets you up, since it gets flagged for the rest of the run, which points at the 6 m² with the 2450. This board helps: its low take-off speed and clean release are what let a 100 kg rider get away with 6 m² rather than 7.

## Comparison with the KT Drifter 5 Pro Carbon (2026-10-15)

Sources: [KT Foiling product page](https://ktfoiling.com/boards/drifter-5-pro-carbon/), [MACkite size chart and price](https://www.mackiteboarding.com/2026-kt-drifter-5-pro-carbon/), [Hoenalu EU listing](https://hoenalu.com/en/product/kt/drifter-5-pro-carbon), [surf-magazin test of the 100 L Carbon](https://www.surf-magazin.de/en/wingsurfing/wing-boards/wingboards-2026-easy-freerider-the-kt-drifter-5-carbon-put-to-the-test/).

First the category point: the Drifter 5 is KT's all-round wing freeride board (stability and width underfoot, Foil Assist, beginner prone and SUP). KT's downwind board is the Ginxu. The nearest Drifter to ours is the 115 L.

| | KT Drifter 5 Pro Carbon 115 L | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Length × width | 190.5 × 70.5 cm | 200 × 56 cm |
| Length/width | 2.7 | 3.6 |
| Volume | 115 L | 120 L |
| Thickness | not published; about 110 mm mean, 140 max by my estimate | 123 mean, 154 max |
| Plan area | about 1.05 m² | 0.98 m² |
| Weight, listed | 6.6 kg (the 100 L Carbon listed at 6.2 kg weighed 7.0 kg in the surf-magazin test, so expect listed +0.5 to 0.8 for a real board; Pro is 0.6 to 0.8 lighter than Carbon) | 6.8 kg budget with pad, 6.35 without |
| Bottom | subtle double concave displacement hull into tri-plane chines, defined tail chines | flat into bevel chines (the same tri-plane idea), hard aft chines, soft-V nose |
| Rocker | reduced entry, flatter tail | 112 mm nose, zero tail, straight run to 1000 mm |
| Rails | almond nose, boxy mid, bevelled tri-plane tail | round soft-V nose, boxy mid with vertical band, bevelled hard-edge tail |
| Tracks | 14 inch (356 mm) | 14 inch (356 mm), adopted 2026-10-16 |
| Construction | EPS with a high-density shell ("Dual Fusion"), biaxial carbon, UD carbon patches, S-glass reinforcements, thin enamel paint | EPS 20 with 3 mm PVC sandwich, 200 g carbon twill, carbon-aramid stance, basalt rail band |
| Extras | strap inserts (V + 1), vent, handle, 2.4 GHz glass windows for Foil Assist | vent, handle, leash plug; no inserts, no windows |
| Price | 2060 to 2230 € | about 1000 € in materials and consumables (see `bill-of-materials.xlsx`) |

What the comparison says:

- **Hydrodynamics.** Ours is 20% narrower and a third longer in length-to-width. At the 6 to 8 kt take-off speeds (Froude 0.7 to 0.9) that is less wetted width and lower wave drag, which is the whole point of a downwind shape. The Drifter buys stability at rest and foil control from width instead; KT's own guidance puts a 100 kg wing rider at the top of the 115 L range. The surf-magazin test confirms the trade: take-off slightly behind a slimmer mid-length, stability and ease ahead.
- **Bottom.** KT's tri-plane chines and defined tail chines are the same design as our bevel chines and hard aft edges. KT adds a subtle double concave for soft touchdowns; we chose a soft-V nose and the chines for the same job and kept the pump zone flat.
- **Construction.** Both are sandwich-style: KT uses a denser shell layer fused to the core, we use PVC. Their biaxial carbon plus UD patches versus our twill plus aramid stance is a wash in weight; per litre we are at parity (57 g/L against about 57 to 60 g/L measured for a Pro). Our rails are tougher (basalt) and their deck is lighter underfoot (no aramid).
- **Tracks.** The 14 inch track is the one thing worth copying. With two foils of different area (2450 and 1950) the 254 mm track leaves little trim range. A 14 inch box needs a 420 mm block and the track range becomes 582 to 938 mm from the tail. Adopted 2026-10-16; the real cost came to 0.3 kg, not the 0.1 kg first estimated.
- **Not copying.** Foil Assist windows (a carbon deck blocks 2.4 GHz; only matters if a Foil Drive is ever planned), strap inserts (0.15 kg; pumping and downwind are usually strapless), paint (the enamel finish is lighter than our clear coat only because it is sprayed thin; a sanded epoxy finish is lighter still).
